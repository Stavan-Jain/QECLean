# The [[360, 12, 24]] bivariate bicycle code

This directory formalizes the code on `ZMod 30 × ZMod 6` with
`A = x^9 + y + y^2` and `B = y^3 + x^25 + x^26`.
The two check matrices each have rank 174, so the canonical stabilizer
presentation has 360 physical and 12 logical qubits. An explicit logical
operator has weight 24.

The lower bound uses two quotient maps, first in the y coordinate and then
in the x coordinate:

```
360 qubits, Z30 × Z6 → 180 qubits, Z30 × Z3 → 90 qubits, Z15 × Z3.
```

A chain is represented by two sheets. Its projected shadow records the
coordinates where the sheets differ. If `a` is the first sheet and `b` the
shadow, then the original weight is

```
weight(b) + 2 * weight(a outside support(b)).
```

The cycle equation becomes a binary affine equation `E a = R b`.
The proof verifies the four physical E and R maps on point masses before
using their mask representations. A flat boundary over a shadow reduces
any other lift to a cycle in the quotient code. This proves the required
intermediate distance bounds and handles the zero-shadow cases.

The physical cycle equation places every bottom shadow in a binary space
generated by 43 certified vectors. A packed trellis gives an upper bound of
12,329,961 vectors of weight at most 22 in that space. The proof checks
274,011 translation-orbit representatives, including zero, their membership,
canonicality and stabilizer sizes. Their disjoint orbits attain the upper
bound, establishing completeness of the census.

The nonzero representatives have 273,982 shallow fibers and 28 deeper
fibers. Empty fibers have dual certificates. Other shallow fibers use a
peeling forest, a complete outside-support census, and a check of every
remaining component assignment. The deeper fibers reduce either to the
1,954 base cycles of weight at most ten or to small affine search trees.
Their lifts reach a common table of 58,130 middle shadows, after translation
and an optional deck exchange. Every physical cycle of weight at most 22
in a top fiber over that table is proved to be a stabilizer boundary.

All computations are certificates checked by Lean's kernel. Packed natural
numbers let one arithmetic operation check many independent lanes, while
generic lemmas connect those lane operations to ordinary binary linear
algebra. The Python generators produce candidate certificates; they are not
part of the trusted proof. X/Z duality and even cycle weights turn the
exclusion of nonboundary cycles of weight at most 22 into the lower bound 24.

The final public object is `stabilizerCodeWithDistance`, with type
`Quantum.StabilizerGroup.StabilizerCodeWithDistance 360 12 24`.
The unconditional lower-bound and exact-distance theorems are
`distance_lower_bound` and `stabilizerCode_hasDistance24`. All three print exactly
`[propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound]` under `#print axioms`.

On an Apple M4 with 16 GiB of memory and Lean 4.30.0-rc2, an independent kernel
replay accepted all 39,227 new safe declarations from 331 modules in less than
807.917 seconds (13 minutes 28 seconds). This conservative, sleep-inclusive
measurement includes process startup, imports, dependency auditing, and object
loading. The replay checked the complete new development against preexisting
library imports, including private proof bodies and both public capstones.
Source and object hashes were verified before and after replay.

Source elaboration is a separate measurement: the fresh configured library build
passed in 2,799.719 seconds of awake elapsed time (about 47 minutes). The machine
slept during that build, but no sleep occurred during the measured kernel replay.
The under-15-minute result concerns independent kernel checking of the proof
objects, rather than rebuilding all source files.
